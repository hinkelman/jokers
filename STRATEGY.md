# Jokers — Strategy

Tips for new players. [RULES.md](RULES.md) says what's allowed; this page is advice. It collects
the tips our family gives new players, written up and checked against the board by Claude.

Positions below are numbered as in the rules: each side has track positions 0–17, your barn door
is position 8 and your home door is position 3.

## The two big ideas

### 1. Go out the back door

Going forward from your barn door to your home door is a long trip: 67 steps with 4 players,
103 with 6. Going backward it is only 5. So the fastest way home is usually:

1. **Come out** with a J, Q, K, A or joker.
2. **Back up with an 8.** The marble lands on position 0, three spaces behind your home door.
3. **Go in** with a small card: a 4, 5, 6 or 7 puts it in house slots 1–4.

A 9 works too. Its backward part can move the new marble back 5 onto your home door (then an A
through 5 gets it in) or back 6 to position 2, while its forward part moves another marble.

**Save your 8s** for marbles that just came out. That is where an 8 does the most good.

Where you land matters a little: position 0 is exactly 10 spaces ahead of the barn door of the
opponent on your right, a common hitting distance (see below). Position 2 and your home door can
be reached only by a joker.

### 2. Hitting a teammate helps them

A teammate's marble that gets hit goes to **their home door**, one step from their house. So:

- A **joker onto a teammate's marble** that is far from home saves them a whole trip around the
  board. This is often better than using the joker on an opponent.
- **Coming out onto your barn door** when a teammate's marble is on it does them the same favor.
- **Check first:** if that teammate already has a marble on their home door, that marble goes
  back to its barn. A teammate's marble hit while sitting on its own home door also goes back to
  its barn.

## Staying safe

These are the cards an opponent needs to land on your marble from behind (162 cards in all,
12 of each rank and 6 jokers):

| Spaces ahead of their marble | Cards that can hit you                          | How many |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|----------|
| 1                            | A, 7 (split), 9 (forward part)                  | 36       |
| 2–6                          | that card, 7 (split), 9 (forward part)          | 36       |
| 7                            | 7, 9 (forward part)                             | 24       |
| 8                            | 9 (forward part)                                | 12       |
| **9**                        | **only a joker** (a 9 must be split)            | 6        |
| **10**                       | **10, J, Q, K**                                 | **48**   |
| 11                           | A                                               | 12       |
| 12 or more                   | only a joker                                    | 6        |

The joker can hit at any distance, as long as the opponent's marble doesn't pass its own home
door or its own marbles to get there. Opponents behind you can also hit backward: an 8 from 8
spaces away, or a 9's backward part from 1–8 spaces away.

- **10 spaces ahead of an opponent is the most dangerous spot.** 9 ahead is surprisingly safe.
- **Don't stop on an opponent's barn door** while they have marbles in their barn. A third of the
  deck (J, Q, K, A and jokers) lets them come out onto it, and a 6-card hand almost always has one.
- **Get off your own home door** quickly. Anything that lands there sends you back.

## Choosing whom to hit

- An opponent's marble that is **close to home** is the best target. Sending back a marble that
  just came out costs them only one come-out card.
- 8s and the backward part of a 9 hit marbles **behind** you, which players often forget.
- A joker that hits an opponent also moves your marble a long way forward.

## Filling your house

- Moves into the house need the exact count, and marbles in the house can't jump each other.
  **Aim your first marble for slot 5** so it doesn't block the rest.
- Your marbles can't pass each other, but they can pass any other color. Keep your own marbles
  from lining up behind each other.

## Managing your hand

- **Keep a come-out card** (J, Q, K, A or joker) while you have marbles in the barn. Turns spent
  discarding are the slowest way to lose.
- **Keep small cards (A–7)** for getting into the house, and 8s and 9s for backing up.
- **Plan for forced moves.** If you have any legal move, you must play one, so a hand of big
  cards can push a marble somewhere bad. Having more than one marble on the track gives you a
  choice of where a forced card goes.
- **Watch the discard piles.** There are only 6 jokers. Once a few have been played, marbles far
  ahead of opponents are much safer.

## Finishing

- **Finish with a 7 or 9 if you can.** The rest of the card moves a teammate's marble for free.
- **Finishing first doesn't waste your turns.** Once your marbles are home, you play your cards
  on your teammate's marbles and can bring theirs out of the barn. Getting one player home fast
  is a good team plan.
